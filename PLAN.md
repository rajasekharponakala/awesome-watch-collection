# Awesome Watch Collection — Master Plan

> A free, open knowledge library for watches: every type, from your first beater to grand complications,
> organised from **beginner → intermediate → advanced → pro**, browsable as a website on GitHub Pages
> with a UX that speaks the language of watches.

---

## 1. Vision

1. **One place to learn everything about watches**: mechanical, automatic, quartz, digital, smart and hybrid.
2. **Clear learning paths**, so a newcomer knows where to start and an expert still finds depth.
3. **Structured data** (brands, calibers, references, glossary) rather than only prose, so the site can search, filter and compare.
4. **Community-driven**: anyone can add knowledge with a pull request.
5. **A site that feels like a watch**: the navigation, layout and micro-interactions all borrow horological ideas.

## 2. The Taxonomy: How Every Watch Gets Classified

Every watch entry is tagged along several independent axes, so one watch can show up in many views.

### 2.1 By Movement / Technology (primary sections)

| Section | Sub-categories |
|---|---|
| **Mechanical** | Manual-wind (hand-wound) · Automatic (self-winding: central rotor, micro-rotor, peripheral rotor) |
| **Quartz Analog** | Standard quartz · High-accuracy / thermocompensated (HAQ) · Solar (Eco-Drive, Tough Solar) · Kinetic / auto-quartz · Radio-controlled · GPS-synced |
| **Digital** | LCD · LED (vintage) · Ana-digi (analog + digital) · Rugged/tool digital (e.g. G-Shock style) · Calculator & data watches |
| **Smartwatch** | Full OS (watchOS, Wear OS, HarmonyOS, etc.) · Fitness/sport trackers · Outdoor/GPS multisport · Kids/safety watches |
| **Hybrid** | Hybrid-smart (analog hands + sensors) · Spring Drive (mechanical power, electronic regulation) · Mecaquartz (quartz with mechanical chronograph feel) |
| **Exotic / Specialist** | Tuning-fork (Accutron) · Atomic-adjacent · Mechanical-digital (jump hour, wandering hour) · Concept & art pieces |

### 2.2 By Style / Purpose

Dress · Diver · Pilot / Aviator · Field / Military · Chronograph / Racing · GMT / World-timer / Travel ·
Sports-luxury ("integrated bracelet") · Skeleton / Openworked · Tool & Explorer · Nurse / Medical ·
Pocket watch · Jewellery watch · Fashion watch.

### 2.3 By Complication

- **Basic**: time-only, small seconds, date, day-date, power-reserve indicator
- **Useful**: GMT / dual-time, world time, chronograph (mono-pusher, column-wheel, flyback, rattrapante), alarm
- **Astronomical**: moon phase, annual calendar, perpetual calendar, equation of time, sidereal time, tides
- **Chiming**: minute repeater, grande & petite sonnerie, carillon
- **Regulation showpieces**: tourbillon, carrousel, gyro/multi-axis tourbillon, constant-force, remontoir, fusée-and-chain

### 2.4 By Price Tier

| Tier | Approx. range (USD) | Examples of what lives here |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | < $200 | Casio, Timex, Seiko 5, Orient |
| Affordable | $200 – $1,000 | Tissot, Hamilton, micro-brands, Seiko Prospex |
| Mid-range | $1,000 – $5,000 | Longines, Oris, Tudor, Sinn, Grand Seiko (entry) |
| Luxury | $5,000 – $50,000 | Rolex, Omega, IWC, JLC, Cartier |
| Haute Horlogerie | $50,000+ | Patek Philippe, A. Lange & Söhne, Audemars Piguet, independents |

(Ranges are guidance only. Each entry stores its own price data with a date.)

### 2.5 Other Axes

Country / region of origin · Era (pocket, early wrist, golden age, quartz crisis, revival, smart age) ·
Case material · Size (diameter, lug-to-lug, thickness) · Water-resistance class · Gender-neutral sizing.

## 3. Learning Paths: Beginner → Pro

Each level is named after a horological stage, so the site's own vocabulary teaches as you browse.

### Level 1 — "First Wind" (Beginner)

*Goal: understand what you're looking at and buy a first watch with confidence.*

- What a watch is; anatomy (case, dial, hands, crown, crystal, bezel, lugs, strap)
- Mechanical vs quartz vs digital vs smart: pros, cons, who each one suits
- Reading specs: diameter, lug-to-lug, thickness, water resistance (what 30 m / 100 m / 200 m really mean)
- Straps & bracelets 101 (leather, rubber, NATO, steel, sizing, lug width)
- Setting, winding and daily care
- Budget planning and "first watch" recommendations by budget and style
- Smartwatch basics: ecosystem lock-in, battery, health sensors, privacy
- Common beginner mistakes, and how to spot an obvious fake

### Level 2 — "Automatic" (Intermediate)

*Goal: understand how watches work and start building a coherent collection.*

- How a mechanical movement works: mainspring → gear train → escapement → balance → hands
- Movement families and workhorse calibers (ETA/Sellita, Miyota, Seiko NH/6R, etc.)
- Accuracy: seconds per day, positions, isochronism, magnetism and how to fix it
- Crystals (mineral, sapphire, hesalite, AR coating), lume types, dial finishes
- Case materials: 316L / 904L steel, titanium, bronze, ceramic, gold, carbon
- Brands map: watch groups (Swatch, Richemont, LVMH, Seiko, Citizen, Casio, Fossil) vs independents
- Buying channels: authorised dealer, grey market, pre-owned, auctions, forums
- Collection strategy: "one-watch", "three-watch", themed collections, the "watch box" concept
- Smartwatch deep-dive: OS comparison, sensor accuracy, sports metrics, longevity
- Servicing: when, what it costs, what a service actually includes

### Level 3 — "Complication" (Advanced)

*Goal: evaluate watches like a connoisseur.*

- Complications explained mechanically (chronograph architectures, GMT types, calendars)
- Finishing: perlage, Côtes de Genève, anglage, black polish, guilloché, enamel, engraving
- Certifications & standards: COSC, METAS / Master Chronometer, Geneva Seal, Qualité Fleurier, ISO 6425 (diver), ISO 764 (anti-magnetic), JIS
- In-house vs modified vs ébauche; vertical integration
- Vintage collecting: originality, patina, service dials, redials, Frankenwatches, documentation (box & papers, extracts)
- Authentication in depth; superfakes and how experts catch them
- Market dynamics: waitlists, secondary premiums, discontinued references, limited editions
- Watch history: pocket watch era, WWI/WWII, the space race, the quartz crisis, the mechanical revival, the smartwatch era
- Regional schools: Swiss, German (Glashütte), Japanese, British, French, American, Chinese, Indian (e.g. HMT), Russian/Soviet
- Micro-brands and the independent scene

### Level 4 — "Grande Complication" (Pro)

*Goal: watchmaking-level knowledge, deep research and expert collecting.*

- Escapement theory: lever, detent, co-axial, silicon escapements, high-frequency, constant-force mechanisms
- Tourbillons, carrousels, remontoirs, fusée-and-chain
- Chiming mechanisms and acoustic engineering
- Materials science: silicon hairsprings, Nivarox alloys, ceramics, sapphire cases, coatings (DLC, PVD)
- Hands-on watchmaking: tools, servicing a simple movement, regulation with a timegrapher, modding (e.g. Seiko mods)
- Watchmaking education: WOSTEP, SAWTA, schools, apprenticeships, career paths
- Independent watchmaking and the AHCI
- Auction-level research: reference histories, serial dating, archives, provenance
- Investment, insurance, storage and estate planning for collections
- Smart/hybrid engineering: low-power displays, sensor fusion, battery chemistry, firmware longevity
- Industry: supply chain, Swiss-made rules, the economics of the watch business

## 4. Knowledge Library: What Gets Stored

### 4.1 Content Types

| Type | Format | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Guides** | Markdown | Long-form learning articles, tagged by level |
| **Movements / Calibers** | YAML data + Markdown | Caliber specs: frequency, jewels, power reserve, functions, users |
| **Brands** | YAML data + Markdown | Founding, country, group, history, notable models, price tier |
| **References (watches)** | YAML data + Markdown | Specific models: specs, variants, release years, price history |
| **Glossary** | YAML | Every term (A–Z), with short + long definition and related terms |
| **Timeline** | YAML | Dated events across horological history |
| **People** | YAML + Markdown | Watchmakers, designers, founders (Breguet, Daniels, Genta, Hayek …) |
| **Resources** | YAML | Books, YouTube channels, podcasts, forums, magazines, museums, schools, tools |
| **Buying guides** | Markdown | "Best X under $Y" lists, kept dated and reviewed |
| **Comparisons** | Markdown | Head-to-heads (e.g. Sub vs Seamaster, Apple Watch vs Garmin) |

### 4.2 Example Data Schema — a Watch Reference

```yaml
id: seiko-skx007
brand: seiko
name: SKX007
reference: SKX007K2
type: mechanical/automatic
styles: [diver, tool]
complications: [date, day]
movement: seiko-7s26
case:
  material: stainless-steel
  diameter_mm: 42.5
  lug_to_lug_mm: 46
  thickness_mm: 13.5
  lug_width_mm: 22
water_resistance_m: 200
crystal: hardlex
lume: lumibrite
certifications: [iso-6425]
years: { introduced: 1996, discontinued: 2019 }
price_tier: entry
levels: [first-wind, automatic]
region: japan
sources:
  - https://example.com/source
last_reviewed: 2026-10-16
```

Similar schemas exist for `brand`, `caliber`, `glossary-term`, `event` and `resource`. They live in
`site/src/content.config.ts` and are enforced on every build in CI, so broken data never reaches the site.

## 5. The Website: GitHub Pages

### 5.1 Tech Stack

- **Astro** static site generator: content collections map directly to `content/` and `data/`, with typed schemas, fast pages and minimal JS.
- **Crown search**: a static JSON index built at deploy time and searched in the browser (no server needed).
- **GitHub Actions** builds on every push to `main` and deploys to **GitHub Pages**.
- Small interactive "islands" (vanilla JS / Preact) only where needed: filters, movement explorer, calculators.
- Light and dark themes, responsive down to phone width, accessible (WCAG AA, keyboard navigation, reduced-motion support).

### 5.2 UX in Watch Language

The whole site is designed as a watch. Each concept maps to a part of the UI:

| Watch concept | Site element |
|---|---|
| **The Dial** | Home page: a large interactive watch face. The 12 hour markers are the 12 main sections; hovering a marker moves the hands to point at it |
| **The Crown** | Global search & command palette (`⌘K`). "Pull the crown" to search, "wind" to explore at random |
| **The Bezel** | Rotating filter ring on catalogue pages: turn it to filter by type, style, price or level |
| **Hour markers / indices** | Primary navigation |
| **Power-reserve indicator** | Reading progress bar on articles (full = start, empty = finished) |
| **Jewels** | Difficulty badges: 💎 to 💎💎💎💎 (First Wind → Grande Complication) |
| **Beats (vph)** | Reading time shown as "≈ 4 min · 28,800 beats" |
| **Lume** | Dark mode toggle. The UI "glows" like Super-LumiNova |
| **Date window** | "Today in watch history" widget on the home page |
| **Complications** | Sub-dials on each page: related links, glossary hits, "next lesson" |
| **Movement / Caseback** | The "About / How this site works" page, showing its own "movement" (architecture) |
| **Calibers** | Movement database section |
| **References** | Individual watch pages, laid out like a spec sheet / warranty card |
| **Watch Box** | Personal wishlist / collection builder saved in the browser |
| **Wrist Shot** | Size visualiser: see a watch at true scale against your wrist size |
| **Chronograph** | Interactive history timeline: start / stop / reset the eras |
| **GMT hand** | World map of watchmaking regions and brands |
| **Service Interval** | Changelog / "last reviewed" stamp on every page |
| **The Escapement** | Contribution guide: how small contributions keep the whole thing ticking |
| **Hacking seconds** | Pause all animations (accessibility setting) |
| **Out of Beat** | 404 page: a watch with stopped hands. "This page has lost its power reserve." |
| **Sweep seconds** | Loading indicator |

**Visual language**: dial-inspired typography (clean sans for text, railroad-track minute scales as dividers),
applied-index bullet points, sunburst / guilloché background textures used sparingly, and a dial colour palette
(white, black, "Pepsi" blue/red, "Hulk" green, salmon, panda) as accent themes the visitor can choose.

### 5.3 Key Pages

1. **Dial (Home)**: the watch face navigation, "Start here" by level, today's date window, featured reference
2. **Learn**: the four learning paths, each a checklist the visitor can tick off (progress saved locally)
3. **Types**: Mechanical · Quartz · Digital · Smart · Hybrid · Exotic
4. **Styles** and **Complications** hubs
5. **Catalogue**: filterable references (bezel filter)
6. **Calibers**: movement database with compare view
7. **Brands**: A–Z plus group tree (who owns whom)
8. **History**: chronograph timeline
9. **World**: GMT map
10. **Glossary**: A–Z, with tooltips everywhere a term appears on the site
11. **Tools**: size/fit calculator, accuracy tracker (s/day), water-resistance explainer, budget planner, strap lug-width finder
12. **Resources**: books, channels, forums, museums, schools
13. **Watch Box**: your saved watches and collection balance chart

## 6. Quality & Governance

- **Licensing**: content under **CC BY-SA 4.0**, code under **MIT**.
- **Sources required** for facts, specs and prices; every entry carries `last_reviewed`.
- **Neutrality**: no affiliate links in core content; any sponsorship clearly marked.
- **CI checks**: schema validation, markdown lint, broken-link checker, image size limits, spell check.
- **Templates**: issue and PR templates for "Add a watch", "Add a brand", "Add a caliber", "Fix an error".
- **Labels** by level and section so contributors find good first issues.
- **Images**: only self-made, public-domain or properly licensed images; credits stored alongside.
