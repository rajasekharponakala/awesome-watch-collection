## Case materials

| Material | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **316L stainless steel** | Corrosion-resistant, affordable, polishes well | Scratches; relatively heavy |
| **904L steel** (Rolex "Oystersteel") | Even more corrosion-resistant, high lustre | Expensive to machine |
| **Titanium** (Grade 2 / Grade 5) | ~40% lighter than steel, hypoallergenic | Duller grey; Grade 2 scratches easily |
| **Bronze** | Develops a unique patina | Can discolour skin; needs care |
| **Ceramic** | Almost scratch-proof, light, colourful | Can shatter if dropped on a hard surface |
| **Gold** (18k yellow, rose, white) | Warm, prestigious, holds value | Soft, heavy, expensive |
| **Carbon composites** | Ultra-light, unique textures | Hard to repair |

Treatments like **DLC**, **PVD** and Sinn's **Tegiment** harden or colour the surface.

## Crystals

- **Acrylic / hesalite:** vintage warmth and distortion, shatterproof, scratches but polishes out.
- **Mineral glass:** cheap and fairly tough, but scratches can't be polished out.
- **Hardlex:** Seiko's hardened mineral glass.
- **Sapphire:** extremely scratch-resistant (second only to diamond). It can chip on hard impacts. **Anti-reflective (AR) coating** on the inside, outside or both reduces glare.

## Lume

| Type | Era | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Radium** | 1910s–1960s | Radioactive and harmful. Vintage only, handle with care |
| **Tritium** | 1960s–1990s | Radioactive but low-risk; decays and ages to a creamy colour. Dials marked "T" |
| **Super-LumiNova / LumiBrite** | 1990s–today | Non-radioactive. Charges in light and glows for hours |
| **Tritium gas tubes** | Today | Self-powered tubes that glow constantly for years |

In the **Lume** theme (the night setting on this site) you can see how lume keeps a watch legible in the dark.
