## When to service

Manufacturers recommend intervals of **5 to 10 years** for mechanical watches. Signs that your watch needs attention sooner:

- The rate has changed a lot, or the power reserve has dropped
- Winding feels gritty, or the date changes at the wrong time
- Condensation appears under the crystal
- The watch has had a hard knock

## What a full service involves

1. The movement is **fully disassembled**, often into more than 100 parts.
2. Parts are **cleaned** in ultrasonic baths and inspected; worn parts are replaced.
3. The movement is **reassembled and lubricated** with several specific oils and greases.
4. It is **regulated** on a timegrapher in multiple positions.
5. **Gaskets** are replaced and the case is **pressure-tested**.
6. Optionally, the case and bracelet are **refinished**. For vintage watches, **say no to polishing**, because it removes metal and value.

## Cost

Expect a simple automatic to cost roughly $150–$400 at an independent watchmaker, and luxury brands often $500–$1,000 or more at the manufacturer, more for chronographs and complications.

## Brand service or independent?

- **Brand service centre:** genuine parts, a new warranty, but higher cost, and they may replace original vintage parts (such as hands or dials) unless you tell them not to.
- **Independent watchmaker:** often cheaper and more flexible. Look for certifications (WOSTEP, AWCI, BHI) and reviews.
