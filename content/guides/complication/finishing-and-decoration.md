Finishing is the craft of making a movement beautiful, even in places no one will ever see. Hand-finishing is one of the clearest differences between a $5,000 watch and a $50,000 one.

## Movement techniques

- **Côtes de Genève (Geneva stripes):** parallel waves across plates and rotors.
- **Perlage:** overlapping circular grains, often on the main plate and under the dial.
- **Anglage (bevelling):** edges are filed at 45° and polished. Look for **inward angles** (sharp internal corners), which can only be done by hand.
- **Black polish (miroir):** steel parts polished so flat they look black from most angles.
- **Straight graining (Côtes droites) and circular graining:** finishing on bridges and wheels.
- **Blued screws:** steel screws heated until they turn a deep blue.
- **Hand engraving:** balance cocks engraved by hand, a signature of Glashütte watches.

## Dial techniques

- **Guilloché:** patterns cut on a rose engine or straight-line engine.
- **Grand Feu enamel:** layers of enamel fired at around 800 °C. The best dials of all, and the most fragile to make.
- **Sunburst, fumé (smoked) and textured stamping.**
- **Applied indices** with diamond-cut facets, and **Zaratsu** blade-polishing on cases.

## How to judge it

Use a 10× **loupe**. Look for crisp, consistent angles; no tool marks; perfectly clean surfaces. Compare an industrial finish (machine-applied stripes, stamped parts) with true hand-finishing and the difference becomes obvious.
