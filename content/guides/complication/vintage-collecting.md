In vintage, **originality is everything**. Two watches that look identical can differ in value by ten times.

## The originality checklist

1. **Dial:** original, not **redialled** (repainted). Look at the printing under a loupe: is it crisp, and does the font match references?
2. **Hands:** do they match the dial's lume colour and the correct period style?
3. **Case:** is it unpolished, with sharp edges and chamfers? Heavy polishing ruins value.
4. **Movement:** is it the correct calibre and serial for the case? Mismatches suggest a **Frankenwatch**.
5. **Crown, crystal and bezel:** service replacements are common and acceptable, but should be disclosed.
6. **Bracelet:** correct period bracelet with matching end links adds value.

## Patina versus damage

**Patina** (evenly aged lume, "tropical" brown dials, softly faded bezels) can add value. **Damage** (water spots, flaking paint, corrosion) usually reduces it. The line is subjective, so learn by looking at many examples.

## Documentation

Original box, papers, receipts, service history and **archive extracts** (offered by Omega, Longines, Patek Philippe and others) prove provenance and can dramatically increase value.

## Where to start

Affordable vintage is a fantastic school: **Seiko** (5, Bell-Matic, King Seiko), **Omega** Seamaster and De Ville, **Longines** dress watches, **HMT** in India and **Soviet** Vostok and Poljot pieces. Buy from specialists, and ask questions until you are satisfied.
