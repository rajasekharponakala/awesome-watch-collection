| Standard | Body | What it certifies |
|---|---|---|
| **COSC chronometer** | Contrôle Officiel Suisse des Chronomètres | Uncased movement: −4/+6 s/day over 15 days, 5 positions, 3 temperatures |
| **METAS Master Chronometer** | Swiss Federal Institute of Metrology | Cased watch: 0/+5 s/day, resistance to 15,000 gauss, water resistance, power reserve |
| **Rolex Superlative Chronometer** | Rolex (in-house) | Cased watch: −2/+2 s/day after COSC |
| **Geneva Seal (Poinçon de Genève)** | Canton of Geneva | Origin and finishing standards, plus a cased-watch test |
| **Qualité Fleurier** | Fleurier Quality Foundation | Finishing, chronometry and a cased "Fleuritest" simulation of daily wear |
| **ISO 6425** | International Organization for Standardization | Dive watches: water resistance (+25% safety margin), legibility at 25 cm in darkness, a unidirectional bezel and more |
| **ISO 764** | ISO | Anti-magnetic: functions after exposure to 4,800 A/m (about 60 gauss) |
| **Swiss Made** | Swiss law | At least 60% of manufacturing cost in Switzerland, movement Swiss, final assembly and inspection in Switzerland |

## Reading between the lines

- A **chronometer** label means the movement passed a test, not that it is better made than an uncertified one. Some excellent makers simply don't certify.
- **"Swiss Movement"** (not "Swiss Made") on a dial may mean only the movement is Swiss.
- **Dive watches** marked "DIVER'S" (with the depth) must meet ISO 6425. "Water resistant 200 m" alone does not guarantee it.
