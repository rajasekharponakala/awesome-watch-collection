Many collectors eventually want to understand watches with their hands.

## A starter bench

- **Screwdrivers:** a quality set, with sizes from 0.6 to 2.0 mm
- **Tweezers:** brass and anti-magnetic steel (Dumont-style)
- **Loupe:** 3×–5× for work, 10× for inspection
- **Movement holder**, **hand levers** and **hand-setting tools**
- **Rodico** (cleaning putty), **finger cots**, **dust blower**
- **Case opener** and **press** for casebacks
- A **timegrapher** or a phone app
- A clean, well-lit desk with a **white or green mat**

## Your first movement

Buy an inexpensive **Unitas/ETA 6497** clone or a **Seiko NH35** and take it apart and reassemble it many times before you touch a watch you care about. Learn the order: **let down the mainspring first**, then remove the balance, then the train.

## Regulation

With a timegrapher, adjust the **regulator index** to bring the rate within a few seconds a day, and the **beat error** to under 1.0 ms. Compare readings in different positions.

## Modding

The **Seiko NH35/NH36 ecosystem** lets you build a custom watch from case, dial, hands, bezel and crystal. It is the most accessible way to learn about fit, tolerances and design.

## Becoming a watchmaker

Formal training programmes include **WOSTEP** (Switzerland), **Lycée/école d'horlogerie** schools, **BHI** (UK), the **AWCI** (US) and brand-run academies. Many independent watchmakers began as hobbyists.

> **Safety:** vintage radium dials are radioactive. Never sand, scrape or open them without proper protection.
