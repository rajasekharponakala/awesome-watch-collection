Changing the strap is the cheapest way to make one watch feel like several.

## Materials

| Strap | Best for | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Leather** | Dress and smart-casual | Water and hot, sweaty days |
| **Rubber / FKM** | Sport, swimming and hot climates | Formal settings |
| **NATO / fabric** | Casual, field and military styles | Very thick cases (they add height) |
| **Steel bracelet** | Everything. The most versatile option | People who dislike weight |
| **Mesh (Milanese)** | Dive and dress watches, vintage style | Hairy wrists (it can pinch) |

## Sizing

- Know your **lug width** (on the spec sheet, or measure between the lugs).
- A bracelet is sized by removing **links**. Many watches also have **micro-adjustment** holes in the clasp, or tool-free extension systems.
- A good fit lets you slide one finger under the strap.

## Swapping a strap

1. Use a **spring bar tool** (or a strap with quick-release pins).
2. Compress the spring bar at the lug and ease the strap out.
3. Fit the new strap's spring bar into one lug hole, compress it and slide it into the other. Listen for the click.
4. Tug gently to check that both ends are secure.

> **Tip:** cover the lugs with a strip of tape while you work, to avoid scratching the case.
