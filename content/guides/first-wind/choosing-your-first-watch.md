Your first good watch is a big decision, but it does not need to be a stressful one. Work through these five questions in order.

## 1. What is your budget?

Decide a number before you browse. Excellent watches exist at every level:

- **Under $100:** Casio F-91W, Casio Duro, Timex Weekender, Casio G-Shock DW-5600
- **$100–$500:** Seiko 5 Sports, Orient Bambino, Citizen Promaster Diver, Hamilton Khaki Field
- **$500–$1,500:** Tissot PRX Powermatic 80, Seiko Presage, Christopher Ward, Sinn 556
- **$1,500–$5,000:** Tudor Black Bay, Longines Spirit, Oris Aquis, NOMOS Tangente

## 2. Where will you wear it?

Office, gym, beach, black tie or all of them? If you want one watch for everything, choose a **steel sports watch on a bracelet with 100 m water resistance**. It works with a suit and in the sea.

## 3. Which type of movement suits your life?

Read [Watch types explained](../watch-types-explained/). If you want to fall in love with watches, a simple **automatic** is a great start. If you just want the time, **quartz** is unbeatable.

## 4. Will it fit?

Use the lug-to-lug rule from [Reading the spec sheet](../reading-the-spec-sheet/). If you can, try it on in a shop, even if you buy elsewhere.

## 5. Where should you buy?

For a first watch, buy from an **authorised dealer** or a reputable retailer. You get a full warranty and zero risk of a fake. Pre-owned is a great way to stretch your budget later, once you know what to look for.

## Common first-buyer mistakes

- Buying too big because it looked good in photos
- Prioritising a famous brand over a watch you actually love
- Buying from marketplace listings that seem too good to be true (they are)
- Forgetting the strap. A new strap can transform a watch for very little money
