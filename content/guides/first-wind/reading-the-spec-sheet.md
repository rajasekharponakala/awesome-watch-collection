A watch's specifications tell you most of what you need to know before you try it on.

## Size

- **Diameter** (e.g. 40 mm): the width of the case, usually not counting the crown. Most people find 36–42 mm comfortable.
- **Lug-to-lug** (e.g. 47 mm): the distance between the lug tips. **This is the most important number for fit.** If the lug-to-lug is wider than your wrist is flat, the lugs will overhang.
- **Thickness** (e.g. 12 mm): affects whether the watch slips under a shirt cuff. Under 10 mm feels slim; over 14 mm feels chunky.
- **Lug width** (e.g. 20 mm): the strap width that fits. 18, 20 and 22 mm are the most common.

> **Rule of thumb:** measure across the top of your wrist with a ruler. Choose a lug-to-lug equal to or smaller than that number.

## Water resistance

The number on the dial is a **laboratory pressure rating**, not a depth you can dive to. Movement in water creates extra pressure, and seals age.

| Marking | What it really means |
|---|---|
| 30 m / 3 ATM | Splashes and rain. Do not swim |
| 50 m / 5 ATM | Hand-washing and a brief swim |
| 100 m / 10 ATM | Swimming and snorkelling |
| 200 m+ | Diving. Look for **ISO 6425** certification |

Always make sure the crown is pushed in (or screwed down) before the watch meets water, and never operate chronograph pushers underwater unless the watch is rated for it.

## Other lines on the spec sheet

- **Crystal**: sapphire is best for scratch resistance.
- **Movement**: the calibre name lets you look up accuracy, power reserve and service cost in the [Calibers](../../../calibers/) database.
- **Power reserve**: how long the watch runs once fully wound.
- **Lume**: the type of luminous material, which matters if you need the watch at night.
