Almost every watch falls into one of six families. The family tells you how it keeps time, how accurate it will be and how you will live with it.

| Type | Powered by | Accuracy | Lives with you like… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Manual-wind** | A mainspring you wind | ±5–20 s/day | A ritual. Wind it every day or two |
| **Automatic** | A mainspring wound by your wrist | ±5–20 s/day | A companion. Wear it and it keeps going |
| **Quartz** | A battery and a quartz crystal | ±15 s/month | An appliance. Set it and forget it |
| **Digital** | A battery and an LCD/LED display | ±15–30 s/month | A tool. Alarms, timers, backlight |
| **Smartwatch** | A rechargeable battery and a computer | Network-synced | A gadget. Charge it daily or weekly |
| **Hybrid** | A mix (e.g. Spring Drive, hybrid-smart) | Varies | The best of both, with some quirks |

## Which one is right for you?

- **You want something accurate, cheap and fuss-free:** quartz or digital.
- **You love craft and the idea of a machine on your wrist:** mechanical, either automatic or manual.
- **You train, run or hike:** an outdoor smartwatch, or a G-Shock.
- **You want one watch for everything:** a 38–40 mm steel automatic with 100 m water resistance and a bracelet.

There is no "best" type. Most collectors end up owning several, because each one is good at something different.
