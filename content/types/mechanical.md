A mechanical watch is a small machine that stores energy in a **mainspring** and lets it out in perfectly measured steps. The energy flows through the **gear train** to the **escapement**, which gives regular impulses to the **balance wheel**. The balance and its hairspring swing back and forth several times a second, and every swing lets the gear train advance a tiny bit. That is the tick you hear.

## Why people still love them

A quartz watch is more accurate and cheaper. People choose mechanical for the same reasons they choose a fountain pen or a vinyl record: craft, longevity and the sense of owning something alive. A well-made mechanical watch can be serviced and kept running for generations.

## What to look for

- **Movement**: a proven workhorse (ETA, Sellita, Seiko, Miyota) or an in-house calibre
- **Power reserve**: 70–80 hours means the watch survives a weekend off the wrist
- **Hacking and hand-winding**: convenient for setting the time precisely
- **Anti-magnetism**: silicon hairsprings or a soft-iron cage resist everyday magnets
